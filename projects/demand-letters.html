AI drafting platform for legal demand letters

Architect and lead · legal · 2024 – 2025

Problem

A demand letter follows the same skeleton every time: the facts of the incident, the injuries, the treatment, the costs, the argument, the number. Attorneys assemble that from case files by hand, for every case, and the work is more transcription than judgment. It takes hours that could go to the parts of the job that actually need a lawyer.

Solution

A drafting platform that produces a first-draft letter from the case material, structured the way the firm writes them, for an attorney to revise rather than compose.

~2× Faster to produce a demand letter with the tool than without it

What I owned

  • Solution architecture and a large share of the implementation.
  • Requirements work directly with the firm.
  • Delivery: what shipped, in what order, and what we told the client about it.

Stack

Python, an LLM drafting pipeline over structured case material, with an evaluation approach for checking output quality against the firm's expectations.

The part worth reading

This engagement ran over budget and behind schedule, and I was on it from the beginning. I am including it because it is the most useful thing I have to say about how I work now.

The technology was not the problem. The problem was that nobody had set expectations clearly with the client at the start — what the tool would do, what it would not do, what "done" meant, and what would happen when the first version was not what they imagined. Without that, every delivery became a negotiation about whether we were behind, and the answer depended on which version of the goal you were holding.

What I changed: I adjusted the technical approach to get something usable in front of them sooner, and I put structured, regular communication back in place so that progress and scope were visible on a schedule rather than discovered at milestones. Delivery steadied, and the engagement finished successfully.

The lesson was not about writing better software. It was that on client work, expectation-setting is part of the engineering, and skipping it is a technical decision with technical consequences. I have run every engagement differently since.
